
## Book Contract

### Core Premise
A comprehensive exploration of the origins of the universe, tracing the evolution of matter from the Big Bang to the formation of complex life, combining rigorous scientific detail with accessible, awe-inspiring narrative.

### World/Genre/Tone
- **Subject**: Cosmology, Astrophysics, and Evolutionary Biology.
- **Genre**: Popular Science / Non-Fiction.
- **Tone**: Analytical, educational, and inspiring. Balances technical precision with lucid analogies.

### Structure & Voice
- **Voice**: Expert, authoritative, yet engaging host (think Sagan or Feynman).
- **Tense**: Present (for concepts) and Past (for historical events).
- **POV**: Omniscient narrator / Educational guide.

### Main Throughline (Key Chapters/Sections)
1.  **The First Three Minutes**: The physics of the early universe, inflation, and nucleosynthesis.
2.  **The Dark Ages & The First Stars**: How gravity sculpted the first structures from the primordial soup.
3.  **Galactic Evolution**: The life cycles of galaxies, black holes, and the structure of the cosmic web.
4.  **The Furnace of Creation**: Stellar nucleosynthesis—how stars forged the elements of life.
5.  **The Emergence of Complexity**: From planetary formation to the chemical origins of life.

### Progression Guardrails
- **Avoid Repetition**: Do not loop back to basic definitions once established. Move from simple to complex systems.
- **Pacing**: Alternate between hard data/theory and illustrative historical anecdotes or thought experiments.
- **Texture**: Use diagrams (described in text), mathematical concepts (explained conceptually), and biographical sketches of key scientists.

### Output Rules
- **Pure prose only**: No markdown outlines or meta-commentary.
- **Heading consistency**: Use `Chapter X: Title` or `Section: Topic`.
- **Continuity**: Build upon previous concepts; do not re-explain "gravity" in chapter 10.
- **No scaffolding**: No "In this chapter we will discuss..."

### Next Chunk Reminder Template
1.  A new scientific concept or discovery (progression).
2.  A connection to a previously explained law (continuity/synthesis).
3.  A real-world analogy or historical context (texture).
4.  A forward-looking question or hypothesis (momentum).
